Key Concepts

Classification of Matter

CategoryDescriptionExamples
ElementPure substance; cannot be broken down chemicallyCu, O₂, Fe
CompoundPure substance; two or more elements chemically combinedH₂O, NaCl, CO₂
Homogeneous MixtureUniform composition throughoutSalt water, air, brass
Heterogeneous MixtureNon-uniform; components visibleSand & water, granite

Physical vs. Chemical Changes

Physical ChangeChemical Change
No new substance formedNew substance(s) formed
Easily reversibleUsually not reversible
Examples: melting, dissolving, cuttingExamples: burning, rusting, cooking

Phase Changes

During a phase change, temperature remains constant — all energy goes to breaking or forming intermolecular forces.

ChangeDirectionEnergy
MeltingSolid → LiquidEndothermic (absorbs)
FreezingLiquid → SolidExothermic (releases)
VaporizationLiquid → GasEndothermic (absorbs)
CondensationGas → LiquidExothermic (releases)
SublimationSolid → GasEndothermic (absorbs)
DepositionGas → SolidExothermic (releases)

Key Formulas (Table T)

$$q = mc\Delta T \quad \text{(heating/cooling a phase)}$$
$$q = mH_f \quad \text{(melting or freezing)}$$
$$q = mH_v \quad \text{(vaporizing or condensing)}$$
$$D = \dfrac{m}{V} \qquad \%\,\text{error} = \dfrac{|\text{measured} - \text{accepted}|}{\text{accepted}} \times 100$$
Water constants: c = 4.18 J/g·°C  |  Hf = 334 J/g  |  Hv = 2259 J/g
⚠️ Common traps: Temperature does NOT change during a phase change. Always check if the problem crosses a phase boundary before applying q = mcΔT.

Practice Quiz

0 / 0
Q1. Which of the following is a chemical change?
Burning produces new substances (CO₂, H₂O, ash) — a new substance is formed → chemical change.
Q2. How much heat is needed to raise 50.0 g of water from 20.0°C to 70.0°C? (c = 4.18 J/g·°C)
q = mcΔT = (50.0)(4.18)(70.0 − 20.0) = (50.0)(4.18)(50.0) = 10,450 J
Q3. During the boiling of water at 100°C, what happens to the temperature as heat is added?
During a phase change (boiling), all energy breaks intermolecular forces — temperature stays constant.
Q4. A liquid has a mass of 36.0 g and a volume of 40.0 mL. What is its density?
D = m/V = 36.0 g / 40.0 mL = 0.900 g/mL
Q5. Which of the following is a homogeneous mixture?
Salt water has uniform composition throughout — a solution (homogeneous mixture).
Q6. An exothermic phase change is
Condensation (gas → liquid) releases heat — exothermic.
Q7. The accepted density of aluminum is 2.70 g/cm³. A student measures 2.59 g/cm³. What is the percent error?
% error = |2.59 − 2.70| / 2.70 × 100 = 0.11/2.70 × 100 ≈ 4.07%
Q8. How much heat is released when 20.0 g of water freezes at 0°C? (Hf = 334 J/g)
q = mHf = (20.0)(334) = 6,680 J released (exothermic — freezing).
Q9. Which of the following represents a pure substance?
CO₂ is a compound — a pure substance with a fixed composition. Air and salt water are mixtures; granite is a heterogeneous mixture.
Q10. Specific heat of water (4.18 J/g·°C) is relatively high. This means water
High specific heat = more energy needed per gram per degree → water resists temperature changes.
