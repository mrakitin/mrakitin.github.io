⚠️ Temperature MUST be in Kelvin! K = °C + 273. At STP: 273 K and 101.3 kPa. Molar volume at STP = 22.4 L/mol. R = 8.31 L·kPa/(mol·K).

Solutions & Solubility

TypeDescription
SaturatedMaximum dissolved solute at that temperature
UnsaturatedLess than maximum; can dissolve more
SupersaturatedMore than normal maximum (unstable)
$$M = \dfrac{\text{mol solute}}{\text{L solution}}$$

Solubility trends: Most solids ↑ with temperature. Gases ↓ with temperature, ↑ with pressure.

Colligative Properties

Adding solute to a solvent (regardless of solute identity) causes:

  • Boiling point elevation — boiling point increases
  • Freezing point depression — freezing point decreases
  • Vapor pressure lowering
Ionic compounds dissociate into multiple ions → greater colligative effect per mole than molecular solutes.

Q1. A gas at 1.0 atm occupies 4.0 L. At constant temperature, what volume at 2.0 atm?
Boyle's Law (inverse): P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ → (1.0)(4.0) = (2.0)(V₂) → V₂ = 2.0 L.
Q2. At STP, what is the volume of 3.0 moles of an ideal gas?
At STP, 1 mol gas = 22.4 L. 3.0 mol × 22.4 L/mol = 67.2 L.
Q3. A gas at 300 K occupies 6.0 L. What volume at 600 K (constant pressure)?
Charles's Law (direct): V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ → 6.0/300 = V₂/600 → V₂ = 12 L.
Q4. A student dissolves 4.0 mol NaCl in enough water to make 2.0 L of solution. The molarity is
M = moles/liters = 4.0/2.0 = 2.0 M.
Q5. As temperature increases, the solubility of a gas in liquid
Gas solubility decreases with increasing temperature (carbonated drinks go flat when warm).
Q6. Which condition causes a gas to behave most like an ideal gas?
High T → fast-moving particles (forces less significant). Low P → far apart (no significant intermolecular forces).
Q7. When salt is dissolved in water, the boiling point of the solution
Boiling point elevation: adding any solute raises the boiling point of a solution.
Q8. A solution contains more dissolved solute than is normally possible at that temperature. It is
Supersaturated contains more solute than the normal maximum — it is unstable.
Q9. How many moles of gas are in a 10.0 L container at 200 kPa and 300 K? (R = 8.31 L·kPa/mol·K)
n = PV/RT = (200 × 10.0)/(8.31 × 300) = 2000/2493 ≈ 0.80 mol.
Q10. Which solution has the lowest freezing point?
NaCl dissociates into 2 ions (Na⁺ + Cl⁻) → 2 mol particles → greatest freezing point depression.
