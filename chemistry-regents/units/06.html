Unit 06: Kinetics & Equilibrium

⏱45–60 min 📊★★☆ Intermediate 🔗Requires: Unit 01, Unit 04

Key Concepts

Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

FactorEffect on RateWhy
Temperature ↑Rate increasesMore particles have energy ≥ Ea
Concentration ↑Rate increasesMore collisions per unit time
Surface area ↑Rate increasesMore particles exposed
Catalyst addedRate increasesLowers activation energy (Ea)

Potential Energy Diagrams

Exothermic: Products lower than reactants; ΔH < 0 (negative).
Endothermic: Products higher than reactants; ΔH > 0 (positive).

Ea (activation energy) = energy of activated complex − energy of reactants.

Catalyst: lowers Ea peak only — does NOT change ΔH or reactant/product energies.

Le Chatelier's Principle

If a stress is applied to an equilibrium, the system shifts to relieve that stress.

StressShift
Add reactant→ Toward products
Remove reactant← Toward reactants
Add product← Toward reactants
Remove product→ Toward products
Increase T (endothermic rxn)→ Toward products
Increase T (exothermic rxn)← Toward reactants
Increase pressureToward side with fewer moles of gas
Add catalystNo shift (equilibrium position unchanged)
⚠️ Common traps: A catalyst does NOT shift equilibrium or change Keq. At equilibrium, reaction is still occurring both ways (dynamic). Temperature changes the VALUE of Keq; other stresses just shift amounts.

Practice Quiz

Q1. Which change would INCREASE the rate of a chemical reaction?
A catalyst lowers activation energy → more effective collisions → faster reaction rate.
Q2. In an exothermic reaction, the energy of the products is
Exothermic: energy is released → products are at a lower energy level than reactants.
Q3. A catalyst increases reaction rate by
A catalyst provides an alternative lower-energy pathway, reducing the activation energy.
Q4. Chemical equilibrium is reached when
Equilibrium = forward rate = reverse rate. Concentrations are constant (not necessarily equal).
Q5. For: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) + heat, increasing temperature will shift equilibrium
The reaction is exothermic (heat is a product). Increasing temperature adds heat (a product) → shifts left toward reactants.
Q6. For: A(g) + 2B(g) ⇌ 3C(g), the correct Keq expression is
Keq = [products]^coefficients / [reactants]^coefficients = [C]³ / ([A][B]²).
Q7. Adding a catalyst to a reaction at equilibrium will
Catalyst lowers Ea equally for both directions → both rates increase equally → no shift in position.
Q8. For: PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ PCl₅(g), increasing pressure will shift equilibrium
Increasing pressure favors fewer moles of gas. Left side = 2 mol gas; right side = 1 mol gas → shifts right.
Q9. An endothermic reaction has Ea = 60 kJ and ΔH = +20 kJ. The Ea for the REVERSE reaction is
Ea(reverse) = Ea(forward) − ΔH = 60 − 20 = 40 kJ.
Q10. Powdered zinc reacts faster with HCl than zinc pellets because
Greater surface area exposes more Zn atoms to HCl → more collisions per unit time → faster reaction.
