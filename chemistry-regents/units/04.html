Molecular Polarity & IMFs

A molecule is polar if it has polar bonds AND is asymmetrical (dipoles don't cancel).

IMF (strongest → weakest)Present in
Hydrogen bondsH bonded to N, O, or F
Dipole-dipoleAll polar molecules
London dispersionALL molecules
Stronger IMFs → higher boiling point, lower vapor pressure.
⚠️ Common traps: CO₂ has polar bonds but is nonpolar (linear = symmetric). Ionic compounds conduct when dissolved or molten, NOT as solids. Hydrogen bonds are between molecules, not within them.

Q1. The electronegativity difference between two atoms is 1.5. The bond is best classified as
ΔEN = 1.5 falls in the range 0.4–1.7 → polar covalent bond.
Q2. What is the molecular geometry of H₂O?
H₂O has 2 bonding pairs + 2 lone pairs → 4 electron groups → bent shape (~104.5°).
Q3. Which molecule is nonpolar despite having polar bonds?
CO₂ is linear — the two C=O dipoles are equal and opposite, canceling each other → nonpolar molecule.
Q4. Which has the strongest intermolecular forces?
H₂O has hydrogen bonds (H bonded to O) — the strongest type of IMF.
Q5. In metallic bonds, valence electrons are
Metallic bonding = 'sea' of delocalized electrons shared across all metal atoms.
Q6. A substance has a very high melting point, is very hard, and does not conduct electricity in any state. It is most likely
Network covalent solids (diamond, SiO₂) are extremely hard and don't conduct — all covalent bonds throughout.
Q7. Which type of intermolecular force is present in ALL substances?
London dispersion forces (temporary dipoles) exist in every substance, polar or nonpolar.
Q8. Hydrogen bonding occurs when H is bonded to
Hydrogen bonds require H bonded directly to N, O, or F (the three most electronegative elements).
Q9. The bond in Cl₂ is best described as
Both Cl atoms are identical → ΔEN = 0 → nonpolar covalent bond.
Q10. An ionic compound in solid state does NOT conduct electricity because
In solid ionic compounds, ions are held in fixed lattice positions and cannot flow to carry charge.
