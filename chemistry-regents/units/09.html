Unit 09: Acids, Bases & Salts

⏱45–60 min 📊★★☆ Intermediate 🔗Requires: Unit 04, Unit 06

Key Concepts

Acid-Base Definitions

TheoryAcidBase
ArrheniusProduces H⁺ in waterProduces OH⁻ in water
Brønsted-LowryH⁺ donorH⁺ acceptor

Water is amphoteric — can act as either acid or base.

pH Scale & Formulas (Table T)

pH = −log[H⁺]    pOH = −log[OH⁻]    pH + pOH = 14
[H⁺][OH⁻] = Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴
pHType[H⁺]
< 7Acidic> 10⁻⁷ M
= 7Neutral= 10⁻⁷ M
> 7Basic< 10⁻⁷ M

Neutralization & Titration

Neutralization: Acid + Base → Salt + Water

Titration: MₐVₐ = MᵦVᵦ

The salt formed takes the cation from the base and anion from the acid.

Strong acids (fully ionize): HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄, HClO₄
Weak acids (partially ionize): CH₃COOH, HF, H₂CO₃

⚠️ Common traps: Lower pH = MORE acidic. At equivalence point, moles H⁺ = moles OH⁻ (not necessarily equal volumes). Choose an indicator whose color-change range (Table M) includes the equivalence point pH.

Practice Quiz

0 / 0
Q1. According to the Arrhenius definition, which is an acid?
HNO₃ produces H⁺ ions in aqueous solution → Arrhenius acid.
Q2. A solution has pH = 3. The [H⁺] is
pH = −log[H⁺] → [H⁺] = 10⁻pH = 10⁻³ M.
Q3. A solution has pH = 5. Its pOH is
pOH = 14 − pH = 14 − 5 = 9.
Q4. In HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻, water acts as a Brønsted-Lowry
H₂O accepts H⁺ from HCl → it is the Brønsted-Lowry base.
Q5. The salt formed from HBr + KOH is
Neutralization: HBr + KOH → KBr + H₂O. Cation from base (K⁺) + anion from acid (Br⁻).
Q6. 25.0 mL of 0.50 M HCl is titrated with 0.25 M NaOH. Volume of NaOH needed?
MₐVₐ = MᵦVᵦ → (0.50)(25.0) = (0.25)(V) → V = 12.5/0.25 = 50.0 mL.
Q7. Which indicator is best for a titration with equivalence point at pH 8.5? (Table M)
Phenolphthalein changes color in the range 8.2–10.0, which includes pH 8.5.
Q8. If [H⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁹ M, the solution is
pH = −log(10⁻⁹) = 9. pH > 7 → basic.
Q9. A 1.0 M HCl solution has a _____ pH than 1.0 M CH₃COOH because HCl is a _____ acid.
HCl fully ionizes (strong acid) → more H⁺ → lower pH than weak acetic acid at same concentration.
Q10. Which substance is amphoteric?
Water can donate H⁺ (acting as acid) or accept H⁺ (acting as base) → amphoteric.
