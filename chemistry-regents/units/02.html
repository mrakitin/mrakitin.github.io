$$\text{Mass number} = p^+ + n^0 \qquad \text{Atomic number} = p^+$$

Isotopes & Atomic Mass

Isotopes: same element (same protons), different neutrons → different mass numbers.

$$\text{Atomic mass} = \textstyle\sum (\text{fractional abundance} \times \text{isotope mass})$$
Neutral atom: protons = electrons. Cation: lost e⁻ (fewer e⁻ than p⁺). Anion: gained e⁻ (more e⁻ than p⁺).

The outermost shell electrons are valence electrons — they determine chemical behavior.

Ground State vs. Excited State

Ground state: lowest energy electron arrangement. Excited state: electron absorbs energy, jumps to higher shell. When it falls back, it emits a photon of specific wavelength → bright-line emission spectrum (unique fingerprint for each element).

⚠️ Common traps: Atomic number = protons (not mass number). Isotopes have same protons, different neutrons. Atomic mass on periodic table is a weighted average.

Practice Quiz

0 / 0
Q1. An atom of carbon-14 contains how many neutrons?
Neutrons = mass number − atomic number = 14 − 6 = 8.
Q2. Which electron configuration represents a ground-state atom?
2-8-2 is magnesium (Z=12) in its lowest energy state. The others violate shell-filling rules.
Q3. Two atoms are isotopes of the same element. They have the same
Isotopes = same element = same number of protons (atomic number). They differ in neutrons.
Q4. A neutral calcium atom (Z=20) forms Ca²⁺. How many electrons does the ion have?
Ca has 20 electrons. Ca²⁺ loses 2 → 18 electrons remain.
Q5. Chlorine has two isotopes: Cl-35 (75%) and Cl-37 (25%). The atomic mass is approximately
(0.75 × 35) + (0.25 × 37) = 26.25 + 9.25 = 35.5 amu
Q6. When an excited electron returns to a lower energy level, it
Electrons in excited state release energy as photons (light) when returning to lower energy level.
Q7. An atom has 11 protons and 10 electrons. Its charge is
11 protons − 10 electrons = net charge of +1 (Na⁺).
Q8. Which subatomic particle has essentially no mass?
Electrons have mass ≈ 1/1836 amu — negligible compared to protons and neutrons.
Q9. The bright-line spectrum of each element is unique because
Each element's unique energy-level spacing produces unique wavelengths when excited electrons emit photons.
Q10. How many valence electrons does sulfur (Z=16) have?
S electron config = 2-8-6 → 6 valence electrons (outermost shell).
